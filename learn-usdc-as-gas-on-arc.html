USDC is the gas token on Arc — and what that breaks

On Arc, transaction fees are denominated in USDC. There is no ETH, no separate gas asset, and no second token for your users to acquire.

That sounds like a pure win, and mostly it is. But it quietly invalidates a set of assumptions that are baked into almost every EVM codebase, and each one costs somebody a day.

The fee floor is 20 Gwei

Arc enforces a 20 Gwei maxFeePerGas minimum. Below it, transactions may sit pending indefinitely or fail outright. This is the first thing that bites people coming from chains where the wallet picks a fee and it just works.

import { ethers } from "ethers";

const tx = await wallet.sendTransaction({
  to: recipient,
  value: ethers.parseUnits("1", 6),
  maxFeePerGas: ethers.parseUnits("20", "gwei"),   // floor, not a suggestion
  maxPriorityFeePerGas: ethers.parseUnits("1", "gwei"),
});

A maxPriorityFeePerGas of 0 Gwei is accepted. A small tip improves inclusion when the network is busy.

Fees are smoothed, so they are predictable

Arc uses EIP-1559 pricing with EWMA smoothing: the base fee is a weighted moving average of recent block utilisation rather than a fresh calculation each block. Short traffic spikes raise the fee only slightly, and it returns to target quickly once load normalises.

The design target is roughly $0.001 per ERC-20 transfer under normal load, with a hard ceiling of 20,000 Gwei bounding worst-case cost.

Why this matters commercially: on most L1s you cannot quote a customer a fixed price per transaction, because you don't know what the fee will be. On Arc you can. That is the difference between "usage-based pricing, roughly" and a real per-transaction margin.

Settlement is final in under a second

Deterministic finality. Confirmed means final — no reorg window, no multi-confirmation loop. If you have waitForTransaction(hash, 3) in your code, delete the 3.

This is what makes Arc viable for agent micropayments: an autonomous system paying fractions of a cent per API call cannot wait six seconds, and it cannot budget against a fee that moves.

What it breaks in your existing code

Assumption Reality on Arc
Gas is paid in ETH Gas is paid in USDC
nativeCurrency.symbol === "ETH" It is "USDC"
Native asset has 18 decimals and is not your payment token Native USDC is 18-decimal for gas accounting and 6-decimal over ERC-20 — same balance
You need a WETH-style wrapper to use the native asset as an ERC-20 You don't; the native token satisfies IERC20 directly
Fee estimates need a buffer for volatility Fees are EWMA-smoothed; volatility is designed out

The decimals row is the dangerous one, and it has its own page: the Arc decimals trap.

Two more that catch indexers

A system emitter logs every USDC transfer at 0xffffFFFfFFffffffffffffffFfFFFfffFFFfFFfE. An indexer watching only the ERC-20 address will silently miss native movements. This is the most common "why is my dashboard wrong" bug on Arc.

Blocklist reverts consume gas without a receipt. A transaction to a screened address reverts and burns gas without producing a receipt you can point at. Your error handling needs a branch for "it failed and I have nothing to show you."
