What 782 x402 services actually earn

Everyone selling you the agent economy quotes the same headline. Nobody quotes the floor underneath it. Both are true, and the gap between them is the whole decision.

This page is the floor, with the arithmetic done, so you can decide before you spend a weekend rather than after.

The headline

From x402.org, last 30 days, fetched 17 September 2026:
Transactions 75,410,000
Volume $24,240,000
Buyers 94,060
Sellers 22,000

That is a real market. It is not a whitepaper. Twenty-two thousand sellers have already turned up.

The floor

From x402-list.com, an independently operated directory that probes every listing and measures what settles, fetched 17 September 2026:
Services listed 782
Endpoints 5,127
Answering right now 660 (84.4%)
Offline for 30+ days 47
Measured buyers, last 30 days 4,778
Measured settlement volume $77,972
Measured settlements 3,119,069

Their own caveat, which is honest and which you should read before quoting the number: this is "a measured floor across 551 listed services... not an ecosystem total", and it is 9.6% of the settlement volume they can measure across every facilitator snapshot they hold.

So: the headline is ecosystem-wide, across all facilitators. The floor is what services listed in the one directory that measures them actually settled.

The arithmetic nobody does

Two divisions turn those numbers into the only figures that matter to you.

Volume ÷ settlements. $77,972 ÷ 3,119,069 = $0.025 per settlement.

The average x402 payment is two and a half cents. That is not a flaw — it is the entire point of the protocol, and it is what makes an agent's per-call budget work. But it has a consequence: to reach $10,000 you need roughly 400,000 settlements.

Volume ÷ services, after concentration. x402-list reports that the top 10 services take 92.1% of that measured volume (as of 2026-09-17 21:38 UTC), and that the single largest listing is 34.8% of it — of which one buyer is 97.2%. That one service is, in effect, a single customer paying itself.

Strip out the top 10 and the remaining 772 services share 7.9%:

$77,972 × 0.079 ÷ 772 ≈ $7.98 per service, per month.

That is the number to sit with. The median listed x402 service earns about eight dollars a month. Not eight dollars per customer — eight dollars total, before you subtract anything.

And most of them stop

84.4% average uptime means roughly one service in six is down at any given moment. 122 of 782 are offline as of the measurement, and 47 of those have been offline for a month or more. Services get built, listed, and abandoned — which is what a market with a $0.025 average ticket and 92.1% concentration does to people who entered it expecting the headline.

What this actually means if you are building

The honest reading is not "x402 is fake". It is:

  1. The rail is real and the buyers are real. 4,778 distinct on-chain buyers in 30 days is 4,778 entities that have demonstrated they will pay a machine, automatically, without an account. That is genuinely new.
  2. A generic endpoint is worth nothing. A "hello world" API that answers 402 competes with 782 other listings for a share of $6,160. Do not build that.
  3. The winners are not selling API calls. The volume is concentrated in a handful of listings — and at least one of them is one buyer paying one seller. Concentration that extreme is not a market finding customers; it is a market finding a few specific relationships.
  4. So the question is not "should I accept x402". It is "do I already have a counterparty who wants to pay me per call". If you do, x402 removes the signup friction and that is worth a great deal. If you do not, x402 does not find them for you — nothing in the protocol creates demand, it only removes the excuse.

Check it yourself

Do not take our numbers. The directory is machine-readable:

curl -s 'https://x402-list.com/api/v1/services' | jq '.services | length'
curl -s 'https://x402-list.com/llms.txt'          # what it exposes to agents

The measured totals are republished with a timestamp on their front page and restated in their State of x402 dataset. If the figures above have moved by the time you read this, theirs are current and ours are not.
